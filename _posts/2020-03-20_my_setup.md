## Working environment

Usually I'm wearing a Bose QC15 headphone with Airmod for bluetooth connectivity.

## How I work

Touching the the trackpad to move the cursor is a flaw I try to avoid at any time.
Keeping the hands at the keyboard gives you all the power you'd ever want.
Well, as long as you don't develop some GUI and need to arrange that element a few
pixels to the left and align it relative to that element's baseline.

I manage the positions of the windows with [Rectangle](https://github.com/rxhanson/Rectangle).
The application windows on my machine are usually arranged like I'd use a tiling window manager. With Rectangle's hotkeys, moving a window to the left half or upper right half is just a key combination press away.

To keep my thoughts or quickly prototype a UI, I swear on a sketchbook (with at least 150g/m^2 paper) and a mechanical pencil with 0.5mm B pencil lead.

I listen to music mixes of electronic music. A favorite source of fresh music
is the BBC Radio 1 Essential Mix. Depending on my mood on the day, I choose one of ~20 alltime favorites.
I regularly listen to the same mixes. The purpose is to not have any surprise moments while getting into **the flow**. I also find Pete Tong's voice very soothing (heya, I've you're reading this, Pete!).

Notifications on my phone do not make any sound or any vibration, nor turn on its display. My watch vibrates when it receives notifications of few important people in my life.
I turned of all system sounds of macOS and disabled the notification center.

# Dealing with files in general

Remember I think taking your hands of the keyboard is a flaw? [Alfred](https://www.alfredapp.com/) let's you get anything done by typing a few characters.
I use it it _all the time_. **All the time**.
I start every app with it. I feel clueless when the launch bar does not show up (happened a few times after rebooting when alfred wasn't launched yet) and having to start an application by double clicking it in the /Applications folder. Like in the Stone Age.

The macOS' Finder app is an ironic joke. I can't find anything with it, it just stands in the way and works bad without moving the cursor.
Luckily, there's [Marta](https://marta.yanex.org/), a two pane file manager, like the Norton Commander in the good old days (not the stone age). Has a fuzzy search/lookup relative to cwd.

Dealing with any kind of compressed files with [Keka](https://www.keka.io/en/) is a breeze. Zero hiccups so far, independent of any archived file I've thrown at it.

# Dealing with audio and video

iTunes with iTunes Match
iTunes is really good in dealing with large music collections. I'm still afraid of the Music.app which comes with macOS Catalina.
Although iTunes Match is slow sometimes it only fails me one matching 'clean' versions of lyrically explicit tracks. It's annoying, but since voices annoy me in music it does not affect me much.

- [Stars](http://www.karelia.com/products/stars/): rate music via hotkey
- [mp3tag](https://www.mp3tag.de/) for fixing id3/apev2 tags. Hands down, mp3tag is a power tool for mass manipulating and cleaning tags. It's outstanding and works well within wine. Nothing comes close to it.
- [XLD](https://tmkk.undo.jp/xld/index_e.html) to transcode audio files, split to tracks while keep the tags.
- [MusicBrainz Picard](https://picard.musicbrainz.org/)
- [Audacity](https://www.audacityteam.org/)
- [ffmpeg](https://ffmpeg.org/) the swiss-knife of audio-video en-/trans-/decoding.
- [youtube-dl](https://github.com/ytdl-org/youtube-dl/) the swiss-knife of leeching from those fancy web2.0 multimedia site
- [beaTunes](https://www.beatunes.com/en/) analyses and inspects your music library and creates smart playlist, finds duplicates, etc.
- [Mixed in Key](https://mixedinkey.com/) anaylses the BPM, key and "power" of your music.
- [Traktor Pro 3](https://www.native-instruments.com/en/products/traktor/dj-software/traktor-pro-3/) is an industry-proven DJ application. I use it with a [Denon MC 6000](https://duckduckgo.com/?q=Denon+MC+6000&iax=images&ia=images).

# Dealing with Images

- Creating SVGs with [Boxy SVG]() is supereasy and efficient. Hotkey all the things!
- [Gapplin]() creates a non-vector, high quality, image of your SVG.
- [Pixelmator](): just a general image editor I do not use so much.
- [PosteRazor](): print large image files to multiple sheets of paper and glue them together.
- optimizing image file sizes: while Tools like Trimage and ImageOptim do not change the quality of an image, [OptImage](https://optimage.app/) does -- unnoticeable. There's still a difference from the before-mentioned two: the file size is usually a massive difference.
