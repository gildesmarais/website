I would like to make a recommendation for the lecture <q>Justice: What's the right thing to do?</q> by [Michael Sandel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Sandel), given at Havard University. It is an introduction on moral reasoning.

A warning in advance: you cannot unlearn what you have learned. This lecture will change your way of thinking and consequently your reasoning.

You can find it on the well-known centralized video platform by that North-American company. The mobile application of the video platform clips the episodes titles unfavorably which makes it hard to navigate. Here are the episodes in order:

- [Episode 01 "The Moral Side Of Murder"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kBdfcR-8hEY)
- [Episode 02: "Putting A Price Tag On Life"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0O2Rq4HJBxw)
- [Episode 03: "Free To Choose"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qw4l1w0rkjs)
- [Episode 04: "This Land Is My Land"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGyygiXMzRk)
- [Episode 05: "Hired Guns"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8yT4RZy1t3s)
- [Episode 06: "Mind Your Motive"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8rv-4aUbZxQ)
- [Episode 07: "A Lesson In Lying"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KqzW0eHzDSQ)

I wish I would have experienced such a lecture format during my studies. The interactivity and open discussions are impressive.[^1] The audience does not shy away from hard questions and is striving to keep discussions on the "high level".

And now, <q>you've got to do what you've got to do</q>: start watching. :)

[^1]: To be honest, I found it weird when the lecturers started to remember the student's names.
