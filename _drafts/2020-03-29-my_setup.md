## Machines and utilities

I prefer a  Macbook (Pro or a full-spec Air) as my main working machine.

A well equipped desk contains at least:

- a screen with 2560×1440px (WQHD) resolution with 109dpi minimum
- the <q>old</q>  trackpad
- the <q>old</q>  wireless keyboard with "International Layout"
- a laptop stand to move the machine above the layer where fluids are placed
- a desk-pad

Usually I'm wearing a Bose QC15 headphone with Airmod for bluetooth connectivity. The headphones are with me for seven years. I use them daily, at least 6 hours a working day. They are in a good state without any defects. Some parts naturally wear out, so I've replaced the ear cushions multiple times and the headband once with after market parts for a few bucks. The headphones cause an aura of silence around me and are super comfortable. They run with one AAA battery (!).

For wireless devices I have a box of rechargeable AA and AAA batteries on me.

To keep my thoughts or prototype a UI, I rely on a DIN A5 sketchbook (with at least 150g/m<sup>2</sup> paper) and a mechanical pencil with 0.5mm B lead.

However, sometimes I prefer working offside a desk. Then I need my headphones, the sketchbook and the computer as a minimal setup.

![minimum working setup](/assets/images/posts/2020-04-05-minimal-setup.jpg)

Staying hydrated and reducing waste is important. I have a reusable water bottle with me most of the time.

## Getting into the flow

I listen to electronic music mixes. A favorite source of fresh music
is the BBC Radio 1 Essential Mix. Depending on my mood on the day, I choose one of ~20 all-time favorites.
I regularly listen to the same mixes. The purpose is to have zero surprise moments while getting into **the flow**. I also find Pete Tong's voice introducing every mix very soothing (I've you're reading this, Pete: Heya 👋).
Listening to anything that requires constant attention or someone talking every few minutes is out of the picture.

You will probably see me grooving and moving to music. This is your optical indicator I'm _in the flow_. The music took over, I hopefully got goosebumps and now I'm _in_.

## Reducing eye and arm strains

Touching the trackpad to move the cursor is a flaw I try to avoid at any time. It's time-intensive and puts a strain on your wrist. Keeping the hands at the keyboard gives you all the power you'd ever want.
Well, as long as you don't develop some GUI and need to arrange that element a few
pixels to the left and align it relative to that element's baseline.

I stand up every hour for a few minutes. My watch reminds me to. While standing up I move the cursor with one swift movement to the bottom left corner. There's a 'hot corner' setup in macOS to lock itself (when returning, my watch unlocks the system automatically <3).

Getting a coffee is usually a good idea in the morning, like doing some exercises/movements is in the afternoon: swirl your arm, circle your head, raise/lower your shoulders, hold yourself on the pull-up bar for a minute, do some pull-ups.

It's also a good moment to look outside and focus something in the distance. Your eyes will thank you.
If you can, get some deep breaths of fresh air and/or air the room. Your brain will thank you.

## Reducing interruptions

Interruptions kill any flow. Some interruptions can be controlled by myself and thus I acted upon them:

- I turned off all system sounds of macOS.
- I disabled the macOS notification center.
- Notifications on my phone make no sound or vibration.
- Notifications on my phone do not turn on its display.

The star in our solar system also annoys sometimes. It's especially annoying as our planet moves around it and thus the position of it changes.
With knowing at which hemisphere I'm located, predicting the direction of movement is easy. This way I can prevent some foreseeable annoyances: direct hit rays and reflections. That's up to be decided case-by-case.

I made one conscious exception: my watch vibrates when I receive a message of a few important people in my life.

If you want any synchronous communication with me (a phone call, a meeting, …) then I advise you to sent a calendar invitation with a fixed date and time. Or become better in typing :)
I also do not send voice messages as I'd not want to receive those, too.

Some interruptions are entirely out of my control: Someone walking to my desk and asking <q>may I interrupt you</q> is a meta-question and … 🥁 … an interruption. While I see it's sometimes unavoidable, I urge you never to do that.
I still don't understand why trams in Berlin are unable to turn (more) silently. I've seen (and heard) trams turning almost silently in other countries (built by the same manufacturer).
I understand blaring sirens of operational vehicles are still required sometimes.
